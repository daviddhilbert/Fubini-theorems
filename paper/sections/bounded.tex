Let the ground be an arbitrary
$(\VF,\Gamma)$-generated ground substructure $\mathbb S$,
with $\Gamma(\mathbb S)$ nontrivial.
All definability remains in the original RV language.
Let $A,A'\in\VF_*$ and let
$f:A\to B$, $f':A'\to B$ be definable maps.
Write $A_b=f^{-1}(b)$, $A'_b=(f')^{-1}(b)$ and
$\mathbb S_b=\mathbb S\langle b\rangle$.
For a parameter base $\mathbb T$, write
\[
 R(\mathbb T)=\sggk(\RES_{\mathbb T})
\]
for the ungraded RES quotient computed with
$\mathbb T$-definable sets, maps and value-fiber relations.
The subscript $\mathrm b$ in $\mathbb E_{\mathrm b}$ denotes
the bounded retraction; the parameter base is specified
separately.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Finite witnesses for the RES quotient.}
The following object-level criterion supplies the finite
data needed for compactness.

\begin{test}\label{test}
Let $\mathbb T$ be a parameter base and
$P,Q\in\RES_{\mathbb T}$.
The equality $[P]=[Q]$ in $R(\mathbb T)$ holds if and only if
there are $C\in\RES_{\mathbb T}$, finitely many ordered pairs
$(L_j,R_j)$, and an actual $\mathbb T$-definable bijection
\begin{equation}\label{finite:RES:witness}
 P\sqcup C\sqcup\bigsqcup_j R_j
       \ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\
 Q\sqcup C\sqcup\bigsqcup_j L_j .
\end{equation}
For each $j$, the pair $(L_j,R_j)$ is either
\[
 \bigl(\alpha_j^\sharp\times Z_j,\
              (\G_m)^{n_j}\times Z_j\bigr)
\]
or its reverse, where
$Z_j\in\RES_{\mathbb T}$ and
$\alpha_j\in\Gamma^{n_j}$ is $\mathbb T$-definable.
All unions and copies are tagged disjoint unions.
\end{test}

To prove the criterion, an element of the ideal defining
$R(\mathbb T)$ is a finite sum of its generators times
elements of $K_0(\RES_{\mathbb T})$.
Write each coefficient as a difference of two positive
object classes. Absorb those objects into $Z_j$, reversing
a pair for a negative coefficient.
The one-coordinate generators already suffice; tuple
generators give the same ideal by expanding a difference
of products.
Thus $[P]-[Q]=\sum_j([L_j]-[R_j])$ in the group completion.
Equality there means that adding one common positive class
$[C]$ makes the two positive sums equal.
Positive scissor equalities assemble into an actual
definable bijection, giving
(\ref{finite:RES:witness}).
Conversely that bijection and the defining ideal relations
imply $[P]=[Q]$ in $R(\mathbb T)$.
This gives finite object witnesses directly.

\begin{thm}\label{fubini}
Suppose that for every $b\in B$ there are
$\mathbb S_b$-definable objects
$\bm U_b,\bm U'_b\in\RV[*]$ and
$\mathbb S_b$-definable bijections
\[
 H_b:A_b\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathbb L\bm U_b,
 \qquad
 H'_b:A'_b\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathbb L\bm U'_b
\]
such that
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm b}^{\mathbb S_b}([\bm U_b])
 =
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm b}^{\mathbb S_b}([\bm U'_b])
 \quad\text{in }R(\mathbb S_b).
\]
Then, over the original ground $\mathbb S$,
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm b}^{\mathbb S}\!\left(\int[A]\right)
 =
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm b}^{\mathbb S}\!\left(\int[A']\right)
 \quad\text{in }R(\mathbb S).
\]
As usual, $B$ and its points are interpreted in the sufficiently
saturated ambient model.
\end{thm}

\begin{rem}
The lifting hypotheses mean
\[
 [A_b]_+=[\mathbb L\bm U_b]_+,\qquad
 [A'_b]_+=[\mathbb L\bm U'_b]_+
\]
in the positive Grothendieck semiring over $\mathbb S_b$.
For these categories of definable sets, finite scissor
decompositions assemble into actual definable bijections;
the displayed maps specify the required witnesses explicitly.
The ground-generation assumption supplies the domain of
the global integral from Section~\ref{sec:setup}.
No generation assumption is imposed on every $\mathbb S_b$
merely to evaluate the displayed RV retractions.



Whenever $\mathbb S_b$ is $(\VF,\Gamma)$-generated, the fiber
integral of Section~\ref{sec:setup} is available and the hypothesis can
equivalently be written
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm b}^{\mathbb S_b}
       \!\left(\int[A_b]\right)
 =
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm b}^{\mathbb S_b}
       \!\left(\int[A'_b]\right).
\]
This applies, for example, to a base parameter $b$ consisting
of VF and Gamma coordinates.
If $B$ is definably bijective over $\mathbb S$ to a set of such
tuples, transport the parameter along that bijection:
the two parameter bases have the same definable closure,
so their definable categories and the stated equalities agree.
This does not assert equality of their generated substructures.
\end{rem}

\begin{proof}
We give the proof when each representative has a single label degree
and nonzero labels; the general case is Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed}(1).
Include in each finite package the complete graphs of both positive
lift bijections $H_b,H'_b$, the representative objects, their label maps,
and their finite-fibre bounds. Validity requires that these graphs define
actual bijections with the displayed fibres and lifts. The formulas for
these data are fixed together with the residue-relation witnesses below.
\medskip
\noindent\emph{Choose the full packages before preparing the base.}
Fix $b\in B$ in the original parameter
coordinates, and put $\mathbb S_b=\mathbb S\langle b\rangle$.
Apply Lemma~\ref{coh:decom} to the two RV representatives over
$\mathbb S_b$, with their zero strata and finite tags retained.
The standing nontriviality of the ground Gamma group
also holds over $\mathbb S_b$.
Only RV decomposition and the RV retraction are used here;
no fiber VF-integration hypothesis is added.

For one such finite package the charts have matrices $M_i$,
free arities $\ell_i$, residue factors $V_{i,b}$,
Gamma supports $I_{i,b}$ and integers
$e_i=\chi_b(I_{i,b})$, and similarly for the primed family.
Set
\[
 C_{i,b}=V_{i,b}\times(\G_m)^{\ell_i},
 \qquad C'_{i',b}=V'_{i',b}\times(\G_m)^{\ell'_{i'}}.
\]
Writing $a^+=\max(a,0)$ and $a^-=\max(-a,0)$, form the
positive RES objects
\[
 P_b=\bigsqcup_i e_i^+ C_{i,b}
       \ \sqcup\ \bigsqcup_{i'}(e'_{i'})^- C'_{i',b},
\]
\[
 Q_b=\bigsqcup_i e_i^- C_{i,b}
       \ \sqcup\ \bigsqcup_{i'}(e'_{i'})^+ C'_{i',b}.
\]
Here $mC$ means $m$ tagged copies of $C$.
The monomial evaluation and the hypothesis imply
$[P_b]=[Q_b]$ in $R(\mathbb S_b)$.
Choose the full finite witness of Test~\ref{test} over
$\mathbb S_b$: the stabilizer $C_b$, the factors $Z_{j,b}$,
the value tuples $\alpha_j(b)$, the orientations of the
pairs, and the graph of the displayed bijection.

Include in the package the representative and label
formulas, their finite-fiber bounds, the monomial source
pieces and coordinate maps, the residue and Gamma factors,
and finite bounds for every RES value support.
Include formulas defining all Gamma-valued parameter
functions and the complete stabilization bijection graph.

The Euler integers have the cylindrical certificates of
Section~\ref{sec:setup}. For every Gamma support, include formulas for a
finite partition into cells, the full finite cell trees, endpoint graphs,
and all projection and ordering conditions. The integer assigned to a
tree depends only on its shape. The covering and disjointness assertions,
and the validity of every tree, are first-order conditions. Thus the
asserted Euler integer is certified over the same parameter base as the
support itself.

Validity of the entire fixed finite package is now a
first-order condition on $b$: all arities and bounds are
fixed, and the required set identities, maps and actual
bijections are given by formulas.
These conditions cover every ambient $b\in B$.
Saturation and compactness therefore give a finite
definable partition of $B$ with one package on each piece.
Here we use the usual small-parameter saturation form of compactness
\cite[Chapter 4]{Marker}.
In particular, the matrices, copy numbers, Euler integers,
arity lists and pair orientations are fixed on a piece.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Preparation with the RV coordinates retained.}
By the standing convention of Section~\ref{sec:setup}, write
$B\subseteq\VF^{d_0}\times\RV_\infty^{q_0}$.
First replace $A,A'$ by the graphs of $f,f'$.
Their projections back to $A,A'$ are definable bijections,
and the graphs still belong to $\VF_*$.
On each compactness piece, write
$\bm U_b=(U_b,p_b)$ and $\bm U'_b=(U'_b,p'_b)$,
with fixed label degrees $k,k'$.
All the RV coordinates of $u\in U_b$, including the finite
tags, remain part of the representative.

Apply the preparation theorem to the finite list of
VF polynomials appearing in the defining formulas for $B$,
all RV/Gamma witness sets, their structure-map graphs, the Euler certificates, the Gamma-valued parameter functions and the stabilization bijection graphs.
These polynomials involve the VF base variables and ground
coefficients; the RV parameters remain free variables.
Thus preparation can be performed on the VF projection of
the base. Carry along the original RV coordinates and every
new RV coordinate introduced by the preparation.
RV contractibility of the prepared polynomials makes all
these RV-sorted data depend on the prepared VF base only
through its RV tuple.

After reparametrizing, and partitioning the zero-coordinate
strata, write the base as
\[
 B^\flat=\{(c,t):\tau=(\rv(c),t)\in S\},\qquad
 S\subseteq(\RV^\times)^d\times\RV_\infty^q.
\]
Here $c$ has $d$ nonzero VF coordinates and $t$ contains
all the retained RV coordinates; the finite stratum tags
are retained as well.
The inverse preparation recovers the original parameter
$b=b(c,t)$.
The representatives and label maps are now denoted by
\[
 \bm U_\tau=(U_\tau,p_\tau),\qquad
 p_\tau:U_\tau\longrightarrow(\RV^\times)^k,
\]
and similarly for the primed family.
Using the transported lift bijections, the first total space
is definably bijective to
\[
 Y=\{(c,t,x,u):
       \tau=(\rv(c),t)\in S,\ u\in U_\tau,\
       \rv(x)=p_\tau(u)\}.
\]
The primed construction is identical.
These total spaces belong to $\VF_*$: they are obtained by
the displayed bijections from the original graph families,
and RV-fiber dimension is invariant under definable
bijections.

Put $\tau=(s,t)$ and form the RV object
\[
 W=\{(\tau,u):\tau\in S,\ u\in U_\tau\},\qquad
 q(\tau,u)=(s,p_\tau(u)).
\]
Its structure map $q$ is finite-to-one.
Indeed, fix $(s,w)$ and choose any
$c\in\rv^{-1}(s)$ and $x\in\rv^{-1}(w)$.
Then $q^{-1}(s,w)$ identifies, by keeping $(t,u)$, with the
RV fiber of $Y$ at the fixed VF tuple $(c,x)$.
That fiber is finite because $Y\in\VF_*$.
The choices of $c,x$ are used only to prove finiteness and
are not parameters in the definition of $W$ or $q$.
Thus $\bm W=(W,q)\in\RV[d+k]$.

There is an explicit definable bijection
\[
 \mathbb L\bm W\longrightarrow Y,\qquad
 ((c,x),((s,t),u))\longmapsto(c,t,x,u).
\]
Its inverse restores the forced coordinate $s=\rv(c)$.
Define $\bm W'$ in the same way, of degree $d+k'$.
Consequently the two global integrals are represented by
$\bm W,\bm W'$ modulo the lifting congruence.
The coordinates $t$ remain RV coordinates; they are not
replaced by VF coordinates or given additional lifts.

The full finite package was fixed before this preparation. Thus its RV/Gamma
formulas, including the stabilization graphs, depend on the prepared VF
coordinates only through $\tau$. Their first-order validity conditions
therefore hold over $\mathbb S\langle\tau\rangle$ itself. The transported
VF lift bijections are retained on the total mixed families. This gives
equality witnesses over the RV base without using injectivity under
extension of the coefficient base.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Euler certificates over the RV base.}
Apply the same preparation to the original-language formulas in the
cylindrical certificates. Each certificate includes the graphs of its
endpoints; it is not merely a list of fibre Euler integers. After
preparation the validity assertions depend on the base through $\tau$
and remain true over $\mathbb S\langle\tau\rangle$.
Their finite cell trees therefore describe the actual Gamma fibres
over that RV base. The shapes and their Euler factors are fixed on a
compactness piece. For any further restriction, refine the finite
certificate partition again before doing a fibre calculation.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{A relative Gamma-push calculation.}
We use the following calculation over the original ground
in place of choosing a common fixed-value residue fiber
of the base.
Let $\bm X=(X,p)\in\RV[k]$, let
$J\subseteq X\times\Gamma^r$ be definable, and put
\[
 J_x=\{\xi:(x,\xi)\in J\},\qquad
 Y=\{(x,z):x\in X,\ \vrv(z)\in J_x\}.
\]
Give $Y$ the finite-to-one label $(p(x),z)$, of degree
$k+r$. Set $g=[\G_m]$ in $R(\mathbb S)$.
The integer function $c(x)=\chi_b(J_x)$ has finite range
and definable level sets $X_e=\{x:c(x)=e\}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{relative:Gamma:push}
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm b}^{\mathbb S}([Y])
       =g^r\sum_e e\,
               \mathbb E_{\mathrm b}^{\mathbb S}([X_e]).
\end{equation}
The sum is finite. All classes in this formula use the
ungraded bounded retraction; the specified RV labels are
retained before that evaluation.
Two finite-to-one labels on the same RV set give the same
bounded value: pad them with constant-one coordinates to
a common degree. The underlying identity is then an RV
isomorphism. Each padding multiplies by the degree-one
point class, whose bounded value is $1$.

Here is the relative calculation, including its unary step.
Work first on fixed nonzero-coordinate strata, carrying all finite tags.
Suppose that $J_x\subseteq\Gamma$ is a point, a bounded open interval,
an upper or lower open ray, or the whole line, with its endpoint graphs
definable over the original ground. Write $\lambda_\bullet$ for its
factor in the Euler table of Section~\ref{sec:setup}.

There is a finite monomial partition of $X$ on which all endpoint
functions depend only on the value coordinates in a fixed rational
Gamma graph. Indeed, apply the value-parameter control in
\eqref{eq:gamma-control} to the endpoint graphs, use their functionality
to obtain finitely many rational affine descriptions, and refine the
monomial partition by their validity loci. This retains the entire
endpoint graph and the ordering conditions on each piece.
On a piece with $h_M(X_0)=V\times I^\sharp$, the lifted cylinder is
$V\times K^\sharp$, where $K$ is the corresponding cylinder over $I$.
One-coordinate Gamma integration gives
$\chi_\bullet(K)=\lambda_\bullet\chi_\bullet(I)$.
The monomial evaluation formulas then give
\[
 \mathbb E_\bullet([Y_0])
   =[V]g^{\ell+1}\chi_\bullet(K)
   =\lambda_\bullet g\,\mathbb E_\bullet([X_0]).
\]
Here $\mathbb E_\bullet$ means the bounded map or the unnormalized
geometric map, as appropriate. Sum over the endpoint partition.

Now let $J_x\subseteq\Gamma^r$ have one fixed valid cylindrical
certificate on a definable base piece. Lift its Gamma coordinates fully
to RV and reorder them so that the next cylindrical coordinate is
adjoined last. The already lifted coordinates and $x$ are retained as
RV base coordinates. The unary calculation applies at every step of
each cell tree. Induction on the tree shows that a cell of shape $s$
contributes
\[
 e_\bullet(s)g^r\mathbb E_\bullet([X]),
\]
where $e_\bullet(s)$ is the product of its Euler factors.
Disjointness and covering in the certificate give an actual partition
of the lifted total family. Summing its cell contributions gives
$g^r\bigl(\sum_s e_\bullet(s)\bigr)\mathbb E_\bullet([X])$.
Finally take a finite partition on which the certificates, hence their
Euler integers, are fixed, and add the resulting identities. This
proves \eqref{relative:Gamma:push}.
Every step retains the RV base coordinate; none of the resulting total
families is asserted to be a RES object over the ground.
Formula~\eqref{relative:Gamma:push} concerns the full RV lift of the
Gamma family, accounting for its factor $g^r$. For the tropical motivic
Fubini formula, see \cite[Theorem 3.1.3]{NicaisePayne}.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Keep the residue families over their RV base.}
For the finite witness packages already prepared over $S$, write the resulting
fiber charts as
\[
 h_{M_i}(U_{\tau,i})
      =V_{i,\tau}\times I_{i,\tau}^{\sharp},
 \qquad \tau\in S.
\]
The matrices, arities, finite tags, label maps and graphs
are part of these packages.
After the preceding parameter control and a finite common
partition of $S$, the numbers
$\chi_b(I_{i,\tau})=e_i$ are constant on a selected base
piece; do the same for the primed family.
The affine map
$\eta\mapsto M_i^{-1}(\beta_{i,\tau},\eta)$ identifies
this free support with the original value piece, so their
bounded Euler characteristics agree.
Retain every source chart on that piece. A chart may have
empty fibers, and its projection need not equal the
projections of other charts.

Put
\[
 \mathcal V_i=\{(\tau,v):\tau\in S,\ v\in V_{i,\tau}\}.
\]
This is an RV object with a chosen finite-to-one label;
it need not be a RES object over the ground.
Apply (\ref{relative:Gamma:push}) with
$X=\mathcal V_i$ and Gamma fiber $I_{i,\tau}$.
The maps $(\tau,u)\mapsto(\tau,h_{M_i}(u))$
retain the RV base coordinate. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{fubini:family:coefficients}
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm b}([\bm W])
   =\sum_i e_i g^{\ell_i}
               \mathbb E_{\mathrm b}([\mathcal V_i]),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm b}([\bm W'])
   =\sum_{i'} e'_{i'}g^{\ell'_{i'}}
               \mathbb E_{\mathrm b}([\mathcal V'_{i'}]).
\end{equation}
The product labels in the calculation can be replaced by
the transported labels of $\bm W,\bm W'$: the monomial
evaluation in the ungraded bounded target is independent
of that label choice, as in the earlier evaluation formula.

There is a useful special case for the later RES relations.
For any definable RV family
$\mathcal Z=\{(\tau,z):z\in Z_\tau\}$ and definable
$\alpha:S\to\Gamma^n$, set
\[
 T_{\alpha,\mathcal Z}
  =\{(\tau,z,u):(\tau,z)\in\mathcal Z,\
                        \vrv(u)=\alpha(\tau)\}.
\]
Its Gamma fibers are singletons, of bounded Euler
characteristic $1$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{fubini:relative:torsor}
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm b}([T_{\alpha,\mathcal Z}])
      =g^n\mathbb E_{\mathrm b}([\mathcal Z])
      =\mathbb E_{\mathrm b}([(\G_m)^n\times\mathcal Z]).
\end{equation}
This treats a varying value-torsor relation directly over
the RV base.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Apply the finite witnesses to the total families.}
For every object family $F_\tau$ use the tagged total
\[
 \mathcal F=\{(\tau,u):\tau\in S,\ u\in F_\tau\}.
\]
Give each total a finite-to-one RV label, for example its
full coordinate tuple when necessary.
These totals are RV objects; no assertion that they are
ground RES objects is needed.
The uniform bijection (\ref{finite:RES:witness}) yields
an actual definable bijection
\[
 \mathcal P\sqcup\mathcal C\sqcup\bigsqcup_j\mathcal R_j
       \ \cong\
 \mathcal Q\sqcup\mathcal C\sqcup\bigsqcup_j\mathcal L_j .
\]
For each $j$, the relative torsor identity gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm b}([\mathcal L_j])
       =\mathbb E_{\mathrm b}([\mathcal R_j]),
\]
with either orientation, even for varying
$\alpha_j(\tau)$.
Apply the ungraded bounded retraction to the total
bijection and cancel the common summands additively:
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm b}([\mathcal P])
       =\mathbb E_{\mathrm b}([\mathcal Q]).
\]
The definitions of $P_\tau,Q_\tau$ turn their difference
into
\[
 \sum_i e_i g^{\ell_i}\mathbb E_{\mathrm b}([\mathcal V_i])
   -\sum_{i'}e'_{i'}g^{\ell'_{i'}}
                   \mathbb E_{\mathrm b}([\mathcal V'_{i'}]).
\]
By the preceding family-coefficient formula this is
$\mathbb E_{\mathrm b}([\bm W])
 -\mathbb E_{\mathrm b}([\bm W'])$.
Consequently the two global bounded values agree.
Since $\bm W,\bm W'$ represent the original total
VF objects under the lifting comparison, this proves
the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{fubini}.
Sum over the finitely many base pieces.
All cancellations above take place in the additive
group of $R(\mathbb S)$; no torus class is inverted.
\end{proof}
